Controlled Observation Sessions

A Controlled Observation Session (COS) is a structured, instrumented sitting in which a small group of qualified observers is present in a controlled environment while an apparatus of interest is monitored. Sessions are conducted under protocol, in the presence of a facilitating observer, and are recorded in full.

Where conventional methods have failed to explain an object’s behavior, the Office applies the documented technologies and processes of earlier investigators, including the séance, and modifies them with contemporary instrumentation in order to obtain new results. The historical record is clear that many séances were experiments, conducted both before external observers and as general demonstrations. The Office does not distinguish between the two.

Protocol (Summary)

  1. The room is designated clean. Wireless devices are excluded for the duration of the session, as a precaution against interference with instrumentation.
  2. Observers are seated. Observers remain seated.
  3. Ambient lighting is reduced to the minimum required for note-taking. The reduction is necessary for instrument sensitivity and is not intended to affect mood.
  4. A facilitating observer, whose role is to allow the agency of the object to be present, directs the session and takes no position on its outcome.
  5. Communication with the apparatus is conducted through several independent channels. Output, if any, is not interpreted during the session.
  6. The session concludes when the facilitating observer states that it has concluded.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this a séance?
The Office does not distinguish between a séance and an experiment. Many historical séances were experiments, and the tools of the spiritualists are technologies. A Controlled Observation Session applies their documented techniques, modified by contemporary instrumentation, to see whether altered conditions yield new results. This experimental mode allows the Office to remain objective in its observations while innovating on earlier theorized understandings of the phenomena.
Do I need to dress in any particular way?
Observers are advised to dress appropriately for a laboratory setting. Laboratory coats are provided to those who ask and, on occasion, to those who do not. Observers may also elect to attend in garments contemporaneous with the original protocol literature. This is known as the period control: replication fidelity is thought to improve when the conditions of the original reports are matched. The Office does not specify the period.
May I bring a candle?
Candles are not part of the protocol. They are, however, permitted.
Will anything happen?
The Office does not make predictions. The Office makes records.

Request a Session

Qualified observers may request a session by completing Form 27-B/6 and indicating Box 14. Sessions are scheduled at the discretion of the Office, and are scheduled further in advance than seems reasonable.
